Is SEO worth it for my business?

SEO can be worthwhile for a business that wants more leads from people finding its services on Google. Its value depends on the work you sell and the cost of the campaign. Leads and sales recorded over time give you a practical basis for evaluating it.

Start with the value of an actual customer

The value of a lead depends partly on the service you sell. A major project, repeat purchase and recurring contract each have a different role in a business’s growth. Campaign costs and recorded leads give you a useful starting point for reviewing SEO.

Capacity affects the result

More calls are useful only if the business can respond and take appropriate work. A company with no appointment availability may not convert the additional interest into sales. Another may have room to grow. The same amount of attention can have different value in those two situations.

Timing affects the decision

Google explains that improvements can take time to influence search results. A business needing immediate revenue faces a different decision from one developing a continuing customer source. Expectations should fit the timeline and resources of the company rather than assume every marketing channel works on the same schedule.

Connect SEO with revenue

SEO is commercially useful when it brings interested people to your business and those leads become paid work. For a contractor, that may be a new project. For a practice, it may be additional appointments. A company providing ongoing services may gain a recurring contract.

Compare the leads and sales recorded for your business with the cost of the SEO service. Include the value of recurring work when that is part of how your company operates.

Compare the objective with the expense

For an illustrative example, a service that leaves $2,000 after delivery costs has different economics from a sale leaving $200. These are examples, not an estimate for your company. The decision should reflect real business figures and realistic expectations, not an impressive traffic total that never becomes revenue.

Revenue and profit should not be confused

Consider an illustrative business receiving $10,000 in new sales. That number does not describe how much money remains after delivering the work. Materials, labor and other costs can make two businesses with identical sales totals receive very different financial value.

The same applies to customer counts. One company may receive a valuable long term relationship, while another receives several small purchases. Neither total can be evaluated properly without understanding what the work contributes to the company.

SEO can support businesses looking for more service requests and new projects. Evaluating the campaign alongside your actual leads and sales gives you a practical view of its value.
