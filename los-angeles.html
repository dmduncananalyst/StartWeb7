The Los Angeles Business Market

Los Angeles brings together international trade, entertainment, technology, manufacturing, healthcare and tourism. Its business market includes companies selling to residents, visitors and buyers outside the city, so attracting customers means understanding which of those groups a business actually sells to.

The City’s 2026 workforce plan identifies that broad industry mix. A company based in Los Angeles might sell a product nationally, accept corporate accounts across the region or depend on customers visiting a single neighborhood. Those are different markets within the same city, rather than one shared audience for every business.

A manufacturer can build its customer base beyond Los Angeles, while a neighborhood business depends on people who can visit or book nearby. The city supports both kinds of business.

Fashion and Manufacturing Beyond Local Retail

The LA Business Navigator describes Los Angeles as an apparel manufacturing center, including clothing factories, sample rooms and design studios that attract buyers from around the world. This is a business market that extends beyond shoppers looking for a nearby store.

A clothing manufacturer can sell production services to brands, while a clothing retailer sells finished products to shoppers. Even within fashion, those businesses are competing for different purchases. A brand evaluating a manufacturer is not making the same decision as someone choosing an outfit.

A design studio may work with brands in other states, while a clothing store welcomes shoppers through its doors. Both belong to LA’s fashion economy, with different ways of bringing in revenue.

Visitors, Events and the Local Economy

The City Tourism Department identifies visitor spending as a contributor to Los Angeles businesses and the local economy. Tourism brings a second customer group into the market alongside residents, including people who are planning a trip and people already visiting.

A restaurant, an event business and a company selling to residents may participate in that economy in different ways. A visitor can plan a purchase before arriving, while an established resident may already have familiar businesses to compare. A corporate event buyer may be purchasing for a group rather than for themselves.

For restaurants and event businesses, visitors can add purchases alongside business from residents. Companies selling ongoing local services may rely much more on the people who live or work nearby.
