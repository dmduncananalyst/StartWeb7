How to Get More Contractor Leads (Pool Builders, General Contractors, ADU Builders)

Contractors generally pull leads from a handful of sources, referrals, past customer reviews, pay per lead platforms, and their own website, each with very different costs and reliability.

Referrals and repeat customers

Strong when they happen, but not something a contractor can predictably scale up when more work is needed.

Online reviews and reputation

A strong base of Google reviews builds trust with homeowners who are actively comparing contractors, and it costs nothing beyond asking satisfied customers to leave one. Reviews alone don't generate new search traffic, though, they mainly help convert people who were already looking.

Pay,per-lead platforms

Angi and Thumbtack connect homeowners directly to contractors, but for a real cost: per-lead fees ranging from $15 for smaller jobs up to $80–$120+ for larger projects like roofing or remodeling, and each lead is often shared with three to eight other contractors bidding for the same job. Review sentiment on these platforms has been rough in 2026, with many contractors reporting leads that never convert and a real cost per booked job that can climb past $1,000.

How StartWeb7 Gets Your Business More Leads

SEARCH

Someone opens the internet browser on their phone or computer and types a question into the Google search bar. They may also open an AI platform, such as ChatGPT or Claude, and ask the question there. They could ask about a service they need or information connected to that service. For example, they might type "How much does an inground pool cost?"

CLICK

Your business appears in the results. The person clicks and enters your website.

LEAD

The person calls your business or completes your form. Their information can be sent directly to your CRM.

POTENTIAL CUSTOMER

You follow up with the lead and have the opportunity to turn that person into a paying customer.

How this compares to paying per lead

This approach doesn't cost anything per lead the way Angi does, and there's no other contractor competing for the same click. The visitor arrives already informed and already interested in that specific contractor's work, which tends to convert differently than a shared lead being fielded by several competitors at once.

Why this actually works: SEO and AEO

When a homeowner types a question into Google, Google looks through millions of websites and picks a few to show first. SEO is making sure your website is one of the ones Google picks, by having the actual answer to that question written clearly on your page.

AEO is the same idea, but for AI tools like ChatGPT. Instead of showing a list of websites, the AI reads your page and just tells the person the answer itself. For that to happen, your page needs to have a clear, direct answer the AI can pull from.

This is the real difference from Angi or Thumbtack. Those platforms sell you access to a homeowner who submitted a request, often to several other contractors at the same time, you're all competing for the same person. SEO and AEO work the opposite way: they bring a homeowner to you directly, already interested in exactly what you offer, with no one else bidding for that same click.
