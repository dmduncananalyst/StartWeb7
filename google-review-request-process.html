A Practical Google Review Request Process

An effective review request process asks real customers to describe their experiences honestly and makes the correct public review link easy to find. It does not buy five-star ratings, filter out dissatisfied customers or replace good service with automated praise. Consistency and authenticity matter more than a burst of reviews collected under pressure.

Ask at a natural moment

A request works best after the customer has had enough time to evaluate the service. For a completed cleaning job, that may be after the client inspects the property. For a multi-stage project, it may be when the agreed work is delivered and reviewed. The message should be short, courteous and clear that the customer is welcome to describe the real experience. Avoid language such as promising a reward for a great review or telling a customer which star rating to choose.

Make the destination unmistakable

Use the business's correct public Google review link. If staff send requests manually, everyone should use the same current link rather than redirecting customers through unrelated marketing pages. A short message can remind customers what service they received without scripting what they should say. The review platform's own interface handles publication, so the business should not claim to review or approve a customer's feedback first.

Follow the platform and consumer protection rules

Google prohibits fake engagement, paid reviews and manipulation of contributions to Maps. The Federal Trade Commission also has rules against fabricated endorsements and incentives conditioned on review sentiment. A business may run a legitimate customer feedback program, but it should not create multiple identities, post on behalf of customers without transparency or suppress truthful criticism. Reviews by employees or insiders can raise additional disclosure issues. The process should be checked against current rules instead of relying on tactics found in an old marketing tutorial.

Treat feedback as operational information

A short review about a missed arrival window can reveal an issue worth addressing. A specific compliment about communication can show what customers value. Responding thoughtfully is useful, but arguments with reviewers or disclosure of private customer information can make a difficult situation worse. Keep responses professional, factual and respectful of privacy. Customers considering a business often look at how it handles an imperfect experience, not only at the average rating.

Reviews fit within a wider website strategy

Reviews are one piece of credibility. People also need to understand the services offered, areas served and how to contact the business.
