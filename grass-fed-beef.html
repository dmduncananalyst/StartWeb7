Grass Fed Beef

October 6, 2026

Grass fed beef can be a healthier choice because it often has less total fat and more omega 3 fats than grain fed beef. The difference starts with what the cattle eat: grass and other forage, or a grain based ration that can include corn.

What do the two labels mean

For a grass fed claim, the U.S. Department of Agriculture describes a diet of grass and forage after the animal is weaned. That can include hay and other plants, not just fresh pasture grass. A corn fed or grain finished animal may have grazed for much of its life before switching to a ration containing corn and other feed. Corn fed beef is different from corned beef, which is beef cured with salt.

Why grass fed can be a healthier choice

What cattle eat affects the nutrients in their meat. Studies comparing grass and grain feeding find that grass fed beef often contains more omega 3 fats and less total fat. Those nutritional differences are the reason grass fed beef can be a healthier option when you are choosing between similar cuts.

The exact amounts vary by farm, cut, and feeding method. Think of grass fed beef as one way to make a thoughtful food choice, alongside the portion you serve and the other foods on your plate.

There is room for both at the table

Corn fed beef is familiar to many families and can have marbling and flavor they enjoy. It also provides protein and nutrients such as iron. Taste, price, and availability all matter when you are shopping. If grass fed fits your budget and you enjoy it, it is worth trying. If your usual beef works for your family, you can still build a balanced meal around it.

What should you look for at the store

If the cattle’s diet matters to you, check exactly what the package says. Grass fed and grass finished describe different parts of the feeding story. A 100 percent grass fed claim is more specific about a forage diet throughout the animal’s life after weaning. If a package is unclear, ask the seller whether the cattle were finished on grass or on a grain ration.

Then compare the cut, weight, and price per pound just as you would with any other beef. You might try grass fed in a favorite recipe, or choose it occasionally when the price works for you. You do not have to change every meal to enjoy the nutritional difference.
