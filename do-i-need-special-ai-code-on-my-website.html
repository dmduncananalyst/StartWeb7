AI SEARCH

Do I need special AI code on my website?

Google says there is no special AI markup required for a website to be eligible for its AI search features. A functioning website and accurate information still matter. Other AI products have their own rules, so a claim about Google should not be treated as a promise about every tool.

AI is not one universal directory

Google AI features and a separate conversational tool are different products. A company can appear in one answer without appearing in another. A piece of code described as universal AI registration is therefore something to question, rather than assume it buys access to every response.

Eligibility is not a reserved place

Being eligible to appear does not mean that every answer will include a particular business. A response may use different sources or discuss only a few options. A technical label cannot explain whether the company was relevant to that person’s exact question.

An explanation should make the claim clear

A proposal for website changes can identify the platform, the purpose and the work included. Clear descriptions make it easier to compare ordinary website maintenance with AEO services.

Business information remains important to customers

A customer following a link still needs to understand what is available and how to contact the provider. Confusing or outdated information creates a problem regardless of whether the person arrived from a search result or an AI answer. The eventual purchase is still a real decision about the business.

Evaluate the response itself

A company name, a source citation and a recommendation are different kinds of appearance. Looking at the actual answer is more informative than counting all three as identical successes. A website visit, a call and a sale then tell you whether that appearance had a commercial result.

Ask what a technical claim actually means

The phrase AI code can describe very different things. It might mean a visible chat feature, an internal software integration or a claim about appearing in generated answers. Those are separate products and should not be bundled into one unexplained promise.

A business owner should be able to understand the purpose of a purchase in ordinary language. Paying for a website feature does not establish that an independent platform will recommend the company. A working demonstration of the feature proves only what the demonstration actually shows.

This distinction matters when evaluating a proposal. A company may reasonably buy software to improve an experience on its own site, but that is different from buying control over external search results. The provider should identify its responsibility and the limits of the product. Clear language protects the owner from treating a technical installation as evidence of a customer acquisition result that has not yet happened.
