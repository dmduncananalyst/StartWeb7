Good Recipes to Try This Holiday Season

October 9, 2026

A holiday meal does not need twelve dishes or an entire day in the kitchen to feel special. A colorful vegetable side, homemade cranberry sauce, and a warm apple dessert can bring plenty of holiday flavor to the table.

Whether you are hosting family or bringing something to a gathering, these three recipes give you something savory, something tart, and something sweet.

Homemade Cranberry Sauce

Cranberry sauce brings a bright splash of color to a holiday plate. This version uses just three ingredients, making it an approachable place to start if you have never made it yourself.

Makes approximately 2¼ cups

Ingredients

  • 12 ounces fresh or frozen cranberries
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 cup water

Directions

  1. Place the water and sugar in a medium saucepan and heat until boiling.
  2. Stir in the cranberries and let the mixture return to a boil.
  3. Lower the heat to maintain a gentle boil. Cook for about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  4. Remove the pan from the heat. Let the sauce cool, then refrigerate until serving.

The berries soften and burst as they cook, creating a sauce with pieces of fruit throughout. Spoon it into a serving bowl alongside your holiday main dish. This recipe is adapted from Ocean Spray’s fresh cranberry sauce.

Roasted Carrots and Parsnips

A tray of roasted vegetables is a welcome addition to a table full of rich dishes. Carrots and parsnips make a colorful combination, with lightly browned edges and tender centers.

Makes approximately 3 cups

Ingredients

  • ¾ pound carrots, peeled
  • ¾ pound parsnips, peeled if needed
  • 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
  • ⅛ teaspoon salt
  • ⅛ teaspoon garlic powder
  • ⅛ teaspoon black pepper

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F. Wash the vegetables before peeling and cutting them.
  2. Cut both vegetables into similar sticks, approximately 3 inches long and ½ inch thick.
  3. Toss with the oil, salt, garlic powder, and pepper.
  4. Spread across a baking sheet in a single layer, leaving space between the pieces.
  5. Roast for 20 to 30 minutes, turning halfway through, until tender and browned.

If parsnips are unfamiliar or unavailable, use the same total weight of carrots instead. Refrigerate leftovers within two hours. This recipe is adapted from Oregon State University’s Food Hero.

Cinnamon Apple Crisp

Warm apples and cinnamon belong at a holiday gathering. This simple crisp has a maple oat topping and does not require rolling out pastry.

Makes 4 servings

Ingredients

  • 4 red apples, cored and diced
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch
  • 2 teaspoons cinnamon, divided
  • 3 tablespoons granulated sugar
  • 1 cup quick oats
  • 3 tablespoons maple syrup
  • Cooking spray for the dish

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Lightly coat a 9 inch glass pie dish with cooking spray.
  2. Toss the apples with the cornstarch, sugar, and 1 teaspoon cinnamon.
  3. Spread the apples in the prepared dish.
  4. In another bowl, mix the oats, maple syrup, and remaining cinnamon.
  5. Distribute the topping over the apples and bake for approximately one hour.
  6. Serve warm, at room temperature, or chilled.

Bring it to the table in its baking dish and let everyone scoop their own portion. The recipe is adapted from Christine Waltermyer’s apple crisp, published by the Physicians Committee for Responsible Medicine.

Make Something You Will Enjoy Too

Before choosing a dish, ask what the host already has planned and whether anyone has food allergies. That small conversation can save you from arriving with the fourth dessert when everyone was hoping for another side.

Try a recipe before the gathering if you have time. You can see how it works in your kitchen and decide whether it belongs on your holiday menu.

Most of all, choose something you will enjoy making. The best part of a holiday meal is getting to sit down, share the food, and spend time together.
