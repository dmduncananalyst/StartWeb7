Evaluating an SEO Agency Before Signing

Before hiring an SEO agency, understand its responsibilities, reporting, account access and the terms for ending the relationship. Clear explanations help distinguish a defined service from promises about Google positions that a provider cannot control.

Understand the assignment before the promise

SEO is a broad service label. It does not tell an owner whether a proposal concerns an existing website, a new site or another part of the business’s online presence. The written agreement should make the assignment understandable without requiring the owner to interpret technical jargon.

The same applies to exclusions. A company may provide website support but not advertising or social media management. Knowing that distinction prevents the owner from expecting work that was never included and prevents a provider from relying on an unclear label.

Ask how success will be discussed

Ask how reporting connects search visibility with leads to your business. A useful report can show website activity alongside recorded calls and appointment requests, with each figure clearly labeled.

Google recommends asking prospective providers about expected results and how success will be measured. The useful answer describes understandable evidence and realistic timing. It should also acknowledge the business’s own responsibilities, such as responding to customers and delivering the work they purchase.

Know who has access to the assets

A domain, a website, an analytics account and a business profile can have separate ownership arrangements. Permission to manage them does not necessarily mean the provider owns them. A clear agreement identifies the ownership arrangement for each asset.

A simple explanation of account control and handover is a reasonable purchasing requirement. If the relationship ends, uncertainty about access can become a larger problem than the marketing result. The agreement should make future responsibility clear while the relationship is still cooperative.

Ask about communication and accountability

A named point of contact makes communication simpler. The proposal can explain who handles the work and who answers questions about progress.

Examples of previous work help you understand the provider’s experience. Ask which services were involved, what the business needed and how the provider reported progress.

Distinguish a commitment from an outcome

A provider can make commitments about the assignment and communication. It cannot decide every Google result or make every visitor purchase. A confident explanation should acknowledge those limits without becoming so vague that the owner cannot tell what is being bought.

A clear proposal makes the decision easier. It explains the work, account ownership, communication and reporting in language you can use to compare providers.
