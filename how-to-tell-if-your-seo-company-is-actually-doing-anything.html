How to Tell If Your SEO Company Is Actually Doing Anything

October 10, 2026

You can judge whether your SEO company is working by reviewing completed work, access to your website’s data, and reports that explain what changed. A useful report connects progress to relevant website visitors, calls, and customers, while distinguishing the work completed from results that take longer to develop.

Paying every month without understanding what happened that month is frustrating. You do not need to become an SEO specialist to ask for a clear explanation.

You Should Be Able to See Your Own Data

Your business should have appropriate access to its website, Google Search Console, and analytics accounts. A company can manage those tools for you without making your own records inaccessible.

Account access lets you verify that reports refer to your business and the dates being discussed. Before changing account permissions, confirm who owns each account and which access level is appropriate. Keeping the relationship organized protects both sides.

A Report Should Explain What the Numbers Mean

A report full of upward arrows is not enough by itself. Ask which pages improved, whether the visitors were relevant to your business, and how the reporting period compares with previous periods. Seasonal demand and unusual events can affect comparisons.

An impression means your website appeared in Google’s results. A click means someone selected it. Neither is the same as a sale. Phone calls and customers are closer to the business outcome, but they need dependable tracking before anyone can attribute them to SEO.

If a report counts every visitor as a customer, or presents estimated revenue as money already earned, the categories need to be clarified.

Completed Work Should Be Documented

You should be able to understand what was completed, what is underway, and what is waiting for your approval. A short work summary can explain this without exposing proprietary processes or overwhelming you with technical details.

Compare that summary with your agreement. The right question is whether the promised work is being delivered and whether its purpose is clear. A busy month of activity is not automatically a productive month, but activity and results should not be confused either.

Results Take Time, Communication Should Not

Google’s hiring guidance says SEO benefits often take several months to emerge. That does not justify silence. Even early in the relationship, you should know the starting point, what has been done, and when progress will be reviewed.

Ninety days without a large rankings improvement does not prove that nobody worked. Ninety days without an understandable work record or agreed reporting is a reason to ask for an explanation.

Be Careful With Ranking Promises

Google specifically warns that nobody can guarantee a number one ranking. Ask what a company means by a promise, which searches it refers to, and how progress will be measured.

A productive review ends with clarity. You understand what you paid for, what the evidence shows, and what still needs time. The most useful reports make those distinctions easy to follow.

Read the Report as a Business Owner

Suppose a report shows 1,240 website visits, 38 phone calls, and 12 new customers. Those numbers describe three different stages. They do not automatically mean all 12 customers came from Google, that every call was a new prospect, or that each visit was a different person. Those are questions for the person preparing the report.

Ask how the numbers were collected and what is included. Repeat customers, spam calls, and visits from employees can make an impressive total less useful. A good explanation makes the definitions clear before interpreting the results.

Compare Progress With the Starting Point

An established website with years of content and a new business website begin in different positions. Competition, demand, and the condition of the site also affect what early progress looks like. A company should explain the starting point it is using, rather than compare your business with an unrelated success story.

Agree on a review schedule and keep the reports together. That makes it easier to see a pattern across several months instead of reacting to one good or bad week. The combination of understandable reporting, documented work, and business results is more useful than any isolated number.
