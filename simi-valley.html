Simi Valley’s Local and Regional Business Market

Simi Valley’s business market includes manufacturing, aerospace related businesses, warehouse and distribution companies, automotive businesses and local commerce. That mix creates both a market for purchases within the community and a market for products sold to buyers elsewhere.

The City’s industry overview identifies these business categories and describes Simi Valley as a gateway between Los Angeles and Ventura County. Its transportation information identifies the 118 Freeway and the Simi Valley rail station.

Those connections help explain why a company can be located in Simi Valley without selling only to Simi Valley residents. A local consumer business and a supplier shipping products operate within the same city but do not depend on the same customer area.

Manufacturing and Distribution Buyers

Manufacturing, warehouse and distribution activity involves purchases that can originate outside the local neighborhood. A supplier may be considered by another business for a product order rather than by a resident planning a visit.

For an owner in that market, the company’s location and the location of its buyers are separate questions. A manufacturer can be based in Simi Valley while selling to customers across a wider region. A warehouse address likewise does not mean the business operates as a store open to walk in shoppers.

For these companies, growth can come from another buyer placing an order or opening an account. Their customer base can extend well beyond the surrounding neighborhoods.

Community Commerce and Shop Simi Valley First

The Chamber’s Shop Simi Valley First program presents a different part of the market, local businesses, special offers, events and community news. It emphasizes purchases made within the community rather than the broader buyer market of an industrial supplier.

A local promotion can introduce an established business to someone who has not bought from it before. It can also encourage a familiar customer to return. Those are both useful transactions, but they are different from winning a new business account outside the city.

A shop can grow through additional visits from residents, while a supplier can grow through orders from buyers beyond the community. Both contribute to Simi Valley’s economy.

Local Customers and Buyers Beyond the City

A Simi Valley supplier can be evaluated by a company outside the area, while a neighborhood business depends on people who can visit or book locally.
